# Model & Mechanism — Target Vertical & First-Customer Research

**Date:** 2026-08-19
**Method:** Six parallel research passes (one per candidate sector), each independently searching Companies House, LinkedIn, trade press, job postings and company websites for UK SMEs (£10M–£100M revenue, 20–150 employees, North of England preferred) showing direct or structural evidence of manual, email/Excel-mediated "translation layer" admin work. Findings below are synthesised from those six passes.

**Read this caveat before anything else:** every research pass hit the same environment constraint — `WebFetch` was blocked for nearly all domains (including Companies House itself), so almost all evidence comes from search-engine snippets, not directly-read primary documents. Companies House revenue/headcount figures quoted below are frequently third-party aggregator estimates (Endole, RocketReach, ZoomInfo, Pomanda, Growjo), which are known to disagree with each other and are sometimes clearly wrong. **Treat every figure in this report as directionally indicative, not verified, unless a source is explicitly described as a hard filing or press-reported filing figure.** Before any outreach, pull the actual Companies House accounts for shortlisted companies directly.

---

## Executive Summary

The strongest vertical bet is **construction plant, tool & welfare-unit hire** — a large population of similarly-structured, independent, family-owned North-of-England operators with a genuinely repeatable workflow (hire/off-hire intake, multi-depot asset tracking, delivery/collection logistics) that incumbent software (inspHire, Syrinx) only partially solves, evidenced by a vendor case study describing a real hire firm's pre-software state as literally "a docket book, Word documents, and Xero," and by the continued 2026 market for "digital T-card" replacements of a decades-old paper-index-card metaphor. The strongest single first-customer bet is **Hather Plant Hire Ltd** (Rotherham) — a family-owned, right-sized plant hire operator with a job posting for a "Plant Controller" explicitly scoped around manually "taking hires and off-hires," sitting inside that same repeatable vertical. The single best-documented piece of pain evidence found anywhere in this research, however, belongs to a different sector: **Dyer Engineering Ltd** (County Durham), whose own Made Smarter case study quantifies the cost of its pre-fix manual shop-floor/ERP gap at up to £10,000/month.

## Vertical Ranking

Ranked by repeatable-market potential — i.e. how many similarly-structured companies likely exist, how similar their core workflow is company-to-company, and how fragmented/inadequate incumbent software is. This is the more important ranking for deciding whether Model & Mechanism builds a point solution or a platform.

| Rank | Vertical | Repeatability verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Construction plant, tool & welfare-unit hire** | **High** | Workflow (hire/off-hire intake, depot-to-depot asset location, delivery/collection/servicing rounds) is close to identical across dozens of independent North-of-England operators. Incumbent software (inspHire, Syrinx/Point of Rental) exists but is priced/scoped for larger multi-branch operators, has genuinely mixed reviews even among payers, and the sector still markets "digital T-card" replacements for a paper-index-card metaphor in 2026 — real, current whitespace. A narrower sub-wedge (welfare-unit/portable-toilet hire specifically) may be even cleaner. |
| **2** | **Logistics, freight & customs** | **Medium-high** | Large regional population (Humber ports, Liverpool, Leeds/Preston corridor) of independent forwarders/3PLs/hauliers. Independently corroborated by a 2026 industry report (Softlink Global, via The Loadstar) stating most forwarders run "operations in one system, finance in another, CRM somewhere else, and Excel holding everything together" — but this session could not obtain a single direct company-specific pain quote (only one near-miss, at Bowker, describing a *solved* past problem), so confidence is more structural than confirmed. Sub-type heterogeneity (forwarder vs. haulier vs. customs agent) means the product wedge may need to be customs-/multi-modal-specific rather than "logistics" broadly. |
| **3** | **Property & facilities management** | **Medium** | Two distinct repeatable sub-workflows — reactive multi-trade maintenance dispatch, and residential block management — each individually plausible (RES Group's own site history hints at home-grown, non-vendor tooling; block management structurally requires juggling many independent contractors across many buildings). CAFM vendors' own marketing ("AI scheduling that replaces spreadsheets and paper job sheets") implicitly concedes the problem is unsolved at the SME tier, but no direct company-specific quote was captured. |
| **4** | **Construction specialists (tenders/compliance)** | **Medium, but fragmented by trade** | The admin shape (re-typing PQQ answers, re-assembling RAMS from an old template, tracking tender deadlines in a personal spreadsheet) is common across all trades and unaddressed by existing procurement software (Procore, Access, Causeway are built for main contractors managing subbies, not for the specialist subcontractor's own admin). But each trade — structural steel, facades, timber frame, scaffolding — has its own unit of measure, accreditation stack, and often its own niche detailing software (Tekla, NHBC systems), so a single generic product would need to be a re-skinnable engine rather than one-size-fits-all. Treat as several adjacent, trade-specific repeatable niches rather than one market. |
| **5** | **Specialist/bespoke manufacturing** | **Medium-low, most heterogeneous** | Pain is real and unusually well-evidenced — the UK government's own Made Smarter programme has published case studies that are essentially pre-packaged admissions of spreadsheets, paper job cards and "lack of integration between operations" at North West/Yorkshire manufacturers. But an aerospace 5-axis job shop, a playground-equipment fabricator, and a bulk-material-handling engineer share almost nothing in BOM structure or quoting logic — which is exactly why the generalist job-shop ERPs (JobBOSS², Global Shop Solutions) exist as configurable platforms rather than fixed workflows. The narrower "subcontract precision machining / job-shop metal fabrication" slice (RFQ-drawing-in, price-out, re-key-into-ERP) is more genuinely repeatable than "manufacturing" as a whole. |
| **6** | **Government/defence SME suppliers** | **Low — deprioritise as a first bet** | Underlying pain is real (old, technically excellent, financially thin manufacturers under heavy certification/traceability burden), but the pool of genuinely independent, under-resourced targets is thinning fast via consolidation (Pearson Engineering → Rafael, Newburgh Precision → X-Cel Superturn, MTL Advanced is already a WEC Group subsidiary). Sector opacity made direct evidence hardest to obtain of any vertical researched. Security clearance, export-control classification of any tool touching technical data, and MOD/prime procurement bureaucracy will likely slow a first sale materially even where the pain is real. Worth 1–2 validation conversations, not the studio's first bet. |

## Top 3 — "Why This One First"

### 1. Hather Plant Hire Ltd (Rotherham, South Yorkshire)
**The case:** Of every company surfaced across six sectors, Hather has the closest match to the brief's own problem signature in a live job posting: a "Plant Controller" role for a "busy plant hire office" whose job is explicitly **"taking hires and off-hires"** — i.e. a person exists whose whole function is manually intaking and reconciling hire/off-hire requests, almost certainly by phone and email. It sits inside the single most repeatable vertical identified (plant/equipment hire), is independently owned (no parent-group IT function to route around), and its revenue/headcount, while imprecisely sourced, is very likely inside the ICP band.
**Suggested angle:** Approach around the off-hire reconciliation step specifically — a lightweight tool that turns an off-hire phone call/email into a structured record without re-keying, sold as a narrow wedge rather than a full hire-management platform replacement.
**Caveat:** Headcount and revenue figures conflict across sources (30–150 range) and need direct Companies House verification before outreach.

### 2. Dyer Engineering Ltd (Stanley, County Durham)
**The case:** This is the single best-evidenced piece of pain in the entire research pass — not inferred, not a job-ad proxy, but the company's own Made Smarter case study, which quantifies the cost of its pre-fix gap between ERP records and shop-floor reality at **up to £10,000 a month**, and describes the fix (real-time location tracking + expanded shop-floor data collection terminals) in enough detail to reverse-engineer a prototype scope. The project was significant enough that Dyer spun out a separate venture (Therion Ltd) to sell similar tooling to other manufacturers — direct evidence that this specific pain is shared, not idiosyncratic, inside the sub-niche of precision/fabrication job shops.
**Suggested angle:** Approach as a narrower, cheaper alternative to what Dyer already built for itself — i.e., "the Dyer fix, productised for a company that can't build Therion in-house."
**Caveat:** Headcount (182) sits just over the stated 150-employee ICP ceiling, and the company already has some internal engineering/systems capability (it spun out a software venture) — worth confirming how much of that capability is transferable vs. sector-specific before assuming "no internal dev capability" holds.

### 3. Fairfax Plant Hire Ltd (Selby, North Yorkshire)
**The case:** The second-strongest plant hire candidate, chosen specifically to sit inside the same top-ranked repeatable vertical as Hather while giving a second, independent shot at first-customer conversion if Hather doesn't convert. Independently (family) owned for 40+ years, growing fast (self-reported £40M+ investment since 2022) in a way that plausibly outpaces its back-office tooling, and its own marketing explicitly frames a dedicated human "hire desk team" — not software — as what "makes the hire process simple," an implicit admission that the interface to the business is a person, not a system.
**Suggested angle:** Same off-hire/hire-desk wedge as Hather — pursuing both in parallel inside the same vertical de-risks the "first customer" bet without diluting the underlying prototype.
**Caveat:** No direct quote of a specific manual workaround (spreadsheet, paper docket) was found for Fairfax specifically — evidence is structural/circumstantial rather than a smoking-gun quote.

## Gaps / Caveats

- **WebFetch was blocked for nearly every domain in every research pass**, including Companies House itself. All revenue/headcount figures not explicitly marked as "press-reported filing" should be treated as directionally indicative aggregator estimates, not verified facts. **Before any outreach, pull real Companies House accounts for every shortlisted company.**
- **"Easy Hire" — resolved, negatively.** The brief flagged uncertainty over a firm possibly called "Easy Hire" in plant/tool hire. Research found the name is generic and unprotected: at least 6–9 separate, unrelated UK companies trade under variants of it (Easy Hire Plant & Tool Ltd, Stockport — now dissolved; Easyhire Plant Ltd, Shropshire; Easy Hire North East Ltd, Hexham/Consett — a genuine micro-business under £1M turnover; Easy Plant Hire Ltd, London-registered; Easy Hire (Plant Hire) Ltd, York; and others). None plausibly reach the £10M–£100M / 20–150-employee ICP band. Recommend dropping "Easy Hire" as a named target in favour of the verified candidates above (Chippindale, Fairfax, Hather).
- **Session-wide WebSearch budgets were exhausted mid-pass** in at least three of the six sector research agents (specialist manufacturing, property/FM, gov/defence, construction specialists all flagged this explicitly), cutting off planned follow-up queries (e.g. Shields (Driffield)'s full Made Smarter case study was located but not read in full; several Companies House financial pulls for ICH Services and Block Management UK Ltd were never completed). These are flagged in the shortlist table as "unconfirmed" rather than omitted, since the structural fit still looked strong.
- **Direct, first-person "we copy that from here into here"-style quotes were rare.** The clearest direct hits came from the Made Smarter case-study programme (Dyer Engineering, Massey & Harris, Robustrack, T&R Precision, Blease Engineering) — a UK-government-funded digital-adoption programme for North West/Yorkshire & Humber/North East manufacturers whose case studies are essentially pre-packaged admissions of this exact pain, written by the companies' own staff. No equivalent single rich source existed for the other five sectors; their evidence leans more on job-posting proxies (hiring volume into coordinator/admin/estimating roles) and structural inference (multi-site, multi-trade, family-owned, no visible self-service portal) than on direct quotes.
- **Several strong-looking candidates were excluded on structural grounds** and are worth remembering as "near misses, not viable": Lambert Engineering (£20M+ revenue but now owned by listed group Mpac plc), Pearson Engineering (acquired by Rafael Advanced Defense Systems), Newburgh Precision (acquired via pre-pack, now JV-owned), Maxim FM (acquired by global group OCS), GB Engineering Nantwich (business closed after founder's death), Stelram Engineering (prior administration — financial fragility risk), TSP Engineering (repeated insolvency, most recently September 2024 — needs a live trading-status check before any outreach).
- **Regional/sector opacity was worst in government/defence** — Companies House access being blocked hurt this sector's research most acutely, and the sector is inherently harder to research due to security sensitivity. Treat sector #6 as the least-validated ranking in this report.
- **No sector agent was able to fully complete its planned research** (all six independently reported hitting either a WebSearch budget ceiling or the WebFetch block) — this report should be read as a strong first pass that materially de-risks a second, narrower research or outreach round, not as exhaustive coverage of any of the six sectors.
